Our declaration

Software has a chimney. It is invisible, it is in your lap, and nobody sends you a bill for it. Every timer that fires while you are reading, every status request that returns twelve bytes and costs a wake-up, every bundle that must be parsed before a button can be drawn — that is fuel, burned somewhere, usually from a battery, usually for nothing.

We refuse the idea that this is the price of a rich interface. Green software is not a feature you bolt on; it is a budget you respect. A plugin that polls four times a second has made a decision on your behalf about how long your laptop will last.

So we keep a ledger. We name the offenders, we publish the method, and we re-run the numbers every single day so that a fix is visible and a regression is not forgotten. 爱护地球，从我做起 — care for the Earth, starting with us. That sentence is not a slogan here; it is the specification.

How to read this read it before you argue with it

Scores are in arbitrary energy units (a.u.). They are a model, not a joule-meter reading: work per day = ticks per day × (payload KB + 1). The constant is there because a twelve-byte response still costs a timer wake, a round trip, a parse and a render — pretending tiny responses are free is the accounting error that makes polling look harmless. Bundles that suspend work while the tab is hidden are credited at half. Server-side cost is out of scope; see the repository for the full method and its limits.
